# UI/UX & Design Guidelines

## Aesthetic: "American Psycho"
The design language should evoke the business card scene from American Psycho. It should feel premium, extremely clean, and focused on typography and subtle textures.

### Typography
- Classic, elegant fonts (e.g., variations of Garamond, Helvetica, or modern sans-serifs depending on user preference).
- Perfect kerning and alignment.

### Colors & Textures
- Minimalist palettes: "Bone", "Silian Rail", eggshell, off-white, deep charcoal.
- Subtle background textures (e.g., subtle paper grain or embossed effects) that feel tangible.

### Animations
- **The Flip:** A smooth, realistic 3D flip animation when transitioning from the front (business card) to the back (links/QR codes).
- Micro-interactions on buttons to give a tactile, premium feel.

### Layout & Navigation (The Hidden App)
- **The Focal Point:** There is no standard bottom tab bar taking up screen real estate. When the app opens, the *entire screen* is dedicated exclusively to the Card. It feels like you are holding a physical object, not an app.
- **The Secret Door:** To access the "regular application" (the Rolodex, Events near me, Settings), the user flips to the back of their card. In the subtle bottom corner, there is a minimalist cog or menu icon.
- **The Transition:** Tapping that icon shrinks the card and transitions the UI into a more traditional application layout (navbar, scrollable lists) where the user can view their calendar, sort folders, and view analytics. 
- *Why this works:* It preserves the absolute purity of the "American Psycho" aesthetic while networking, hiding the complex software UI until the user is alone and actually wants to use the CRM features.
